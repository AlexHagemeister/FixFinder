## Final Report

**Due: Sunday, December 13, at 11:59 PM**\
Submit a **single PDF** on bCourses, named **Team##\_Report.pdf** (for example, Team07_Report.pdf).

The final report is the record of your whole project, from first brainstorm to finished prototype. It's where you capture everything important that didn't fit into the presentation, video, or poster. How did you run your user studies? How did your design change, and why? What did you build, and how does it work? Someone who missed the entire semester should be able to read it and understand what your team did, what you learned, and why your final design looks the way it does. You may use this yourself to remind you later how you produced some of the final designs in this project.

You have complete freedom over the design and layout of this report. Treat it as a designer would. We grade the design of the report itself, so be creative. This is not your fifth-grade report on the Roman Empire, and it shouldn't look like one.

**Show, don't just tell.** This report should be rich with visual material from every stage of the project:

- Photos from your fieldwork and observations: the places, the work, the tools and workarounds your users rely on
- Photos and screenshots from your user studies and testing sessions
- Brainstorming boards, sticky notes, and whiteboard sessions
- Early sketches, including the ideas you abandoned
- Storyboards and scenario illustrations
- Paper prototypes, wireframes, and intermediate designs, ideally side by side so we can see how your design evolved
- Your final high-fidelity screens

Start gathering this material now, if you haven't. Scan or photograph your sketches, and pull together the photos your team has taken all semester. Get permission from users who appears in a photo, and blur faces or crop if they'd rather not be identified. Every image should have a short caption saying what it shows and why it matters.

### What to Include

**Overview**

- Project title
- Team members' full names
- A project summary, **250 words max**
- A link to your team's **Git repository**, with all code and any data, plus the **exact file and location where your API key is used**. Don't include your key; just point us directly to where to insert ours. Include any instructions on how to use it.

**Design process**\
Give each item a brief introduction and summary, and illustrate it with real artifacts from your process.

- **Brainstorming:** photos of your boards, notes, and early idea lists
- **User research:** how you found and observed your users, who they were, and what you saw and heard, with photos from the field
- **Your three primary tasks:** what they are, and what in your fieldwork led you to choose them
- **Sketches and early designs:** intermediate and final sketches, variations, and ideas, including the directions you dropped and why
- **Scenarios and storyboards**
- **Wireframes and prototypes:** from low to high fidelity
- **User studies and testing:** how you conducted them, who participated, what you found, and what you changed as a result, with photos or screenshots from the sessions
- **Competitive analysis**
- **Design evolution:** how your design changed over the semester, and why. Before-and-after comparisons work well here.
- **Final design:** screenshots of your high-fidelity system covering **all three primary tasks**

**AI models and implementation**

- **Which models you used, and for what.** Describe each model call in your system: what it does, what goes in and what comes out, and what it contributes for your users.
- **Why these models.** What else did you try or consider? Why did you choose these over the alternatives, based on quality, speed, cost, or modality? If you switched models partway through, explain why.
- **How your interface handles AI output.** How do you label generated content, and how do you handle slow or wrong responses?
- **Cost report:** what your team spent in total, your single most expensive operation, and what you would change if you needed to lower the cost.
- **Failure log:** specific things a model got wrong during development, and how your interface handles each one now. Include at least 3.
- **Technical challenges**, plus anything someone would need to know to run and use your project.

**AI Usage Disclosure**\
In a short section (about 150–300 words), describe how your team used AI tools while completing this project. We're not asking *whether* you used AI. We're asking you to be designers and computer scientists who can explain and defend every decision in your work, regardless of where the first draft came from. This is separate from the AI your application calls at runtime; it's about the tools your team used to research, design, and build the project. Address:

- **Which tools you used** (for example Claude, ChatGPT, GitHub Copilot, Figma AI features, Claude Code, Midjourney), and for which parts of the project: research, brainstorming, visual design, code, debugging, writing.
- **What you kept, changed, or threw out** from what the AI gave you, and why. This is the part that shows your team was directing the tools rather than the other way around.
- **One thing your team did entirely without AI**, and one moment, if any, where a tool got something wrong, misled you, or wasn't useful.
- **If your team didn't use any AI tools** to build the project, say so directly. That's a completely acceptable answer, and it counts the same as a thoughtful disclosure.

**Summary**

- What is novel about your project, and what value does it offer your users?
