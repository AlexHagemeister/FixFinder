## Final Poster

Your team will prepare a poster presenting your project as a **single PDF** that prints at **30" × 40"**, either horizontal or vertical. Submit only a PDF, with all fonts embedded. Producing a print-ready file is a skill you will need as a designer.

Your poster should stand on its own in describing your project. During the poster session at the public showcase on **Thursday, December 10**, your team will also stand next to your poster and present it.

As designers, you are free to choose how to communicate your idea and represent your project. In past semesters we gave students a list of what to include, and many copied it verbatim onto their posters, which made for boring posters. We also gave out a template, and everyone ended up using the same one. This is a design class. We expect you to communicate your design in a compelling, clear, and creative way. You've seen many examples of great design throughout the semester.

A few reminders:

- **Do include** your team members' names, your target users, the problem and your solution, how your design evolved, and your final prototype.
- **Call out obvious competitors** clearly, if there are any.
- **Limit text.** Use visual elements to communicate your design wherever you can. How often have you read all the text on a poster? Find visual ways to cut text without losing content.
- **Include images** that show elements of your final design. Imagine you are standing in front of your poster describing your final project. Think of what images you may want to point to as you describe your project.

Someone unfamiliar with your project should be able to grasp the gist of it in **under 30 seconds**. Well-designed posters draw people in and invite further conversation.

We devoted an entire lecture to typography and visual design, and we will grade posters partly on how well you apply the concepts from that lecture.

**We will print all posters for you. Because printing 20 posters at this size takes several days, poster files are due Sunday, December 6, at 11:59 PM, four days before the showcase. Do not submit your poster late. It will not be printed.**

Submit your PDF on bCourses, named **Team##\_Poster.pdf** (for example, Team07_Poster.pdf).

### Grading

- Does the poster clearly communicate the project? Can a viewer tell what the project is about at a glance?
- Does it use visual elements effectively?
- Does it make good use of graphic design: typography, layout (grid and hierarchy), and color?

Note as UC Berkeley students you all have full assess to the Adobe Suite of software which we recommend for your layout using any of the tools below. Here are completely blank templates you can use to ensure that your poster is the correct size.

  - [poster-landscape.psd](https://bcourses.berkeley.edu/courses/1557527/files/96136594?wrap=1) 
  - When exporting to a PDF, make sure to merge all layers into one single layer and make sure to uncheck the box "Preserve Photoshop Editing Capabilities"

**Before submitting, check that your file meets all of these:**

- **White background.** Well, we want you to be flexible in your designs and we will allow some use of background color. **Please do not choose black**. That will cause us to run out of ink quickly.
- **CMYK color mode.**
  - Illustrator: File → Document Color Mode → CMYK
  - Photoshop: Edit → Convert to Profile → Working CMYK
  - InDesign: CMYK by default
- **Text inside the margins** (the blue outline on the template).
- **Body text at least 32 pt.**
- **Photos at 300 dpi.**
- **Fonts embedded.** Exporting from any Adobe app should embed fonts automatically. To make sure, open the PDF on a computer that doesn't have your fonts installed.
- **Flattened PDF** (instructions below).

### Flattening Your PDF

Unflattened PDFs waste space and take far longer to print, because they're unnecessarily large. Sharing flattened PDFs is a valuable skill.

PDFs exported from layered documents, such as InDesign files, can contain multiple layers on a single page. When a layered PDF is printed, only the layer currently visible on screen prints, which can produce unexpected results. Flattening merges everything into what you actually see.

Flattening can't be undone, so **work on a copy**.

In Adobe Acrobat Pro:

1.  Make a backup: **File → Save As**, and give the copy a new name.
2.  Open the copy and choose **File → Save as Other → Optimized PDF**. In older versions of Acrobat, this is **Advanced → PDF Optimizer**.
3.  Under **Discard User Data**, select **Discard hidden layer content and flatten visible layers**, then click **OK**.
4.  Save the optimized PDF with another new name.

The flattened file should be noticeably smaller but still high quality. That's the file to submit.
